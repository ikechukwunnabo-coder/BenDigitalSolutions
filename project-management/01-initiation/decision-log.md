**Confirmed MVP boundary**
Build in Version 1:

- Accounts and authentication
- Customers
- CRM and lead management
- Services
- Portfolio
- Projects
- Notifications
- Audit logs
Defer to future phases:

- Proposals and quotations
- Invoicing
- Payments
- Digital products and orders
- Subscriptions
- Learning management
- Advanced analytics
Keep the deferred modules in the long-term roadmap, but do not build them or introduce unnecessary infrastructure for them during Version 1.
**Phase 0 execution**
Start with these deliverables:

- Convert the existing charter and management plan into version-controlled Markdown documents.
- Add a formal approval record and document versioning convention.
- Establish the proposed repository and project folder structure.
- Create README.md, PROJECT_CONTEXT.md, CHANGELOG.md, .gitignore and .env.example.
- Record the MVP boundary and deferred scope.
- Establish the decision log and change-control process.
Important: The charter should remain pending approval until you have personally reviewed and signed off on it. Creating an approval record is not the same as approving the project.
**Phase 1 execution**
After reviewing Phase 0, complete:

- Detailed scope statement and requirements.
- User stories and acceptance criteria.
- Task-level WBS with estimates and dependencies.
- Reconciled Gantt schedule and capacity plan.
- Itemised budget and budget tracker.
- Risk register and issue log.
- Stakeholder and communication plans.
- RACI matrix.
- Definition of Done and milestone acceptance criteria.
The previous 140-hour estimate should be treated as provisional until the detailed WBS is complete. The revised delivery sequence also needs to be reconciled with the original 12-week schedule before we establish a schedule baseline.
My recommendation: Do not start application coding until the scope, requirements, architecture and development foundations have been reviewed. That gives you a much stronger project management portfolio because you will have evidence of actual initiation, planning, execution, monitoring and closure—not simply a finished website.
